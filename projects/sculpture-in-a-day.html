METAL / 03

Sculpture In A Day Challenge

WORK
Site-specific scrap-metal sculpture
YEAR
2023
MEDIUM
Found scrap metal · Welding
CONTEXT
Solo personal project · Self-imposed one-day challenge

Overview

I designed and welded a small sculpture of a person reading a book in a tree, using only metal that had been found or discarded. Set beside a frequently used path between a house and shed on a koala habitat reserve, the figure is intended to sit quietly within its surroundings and catch the eye of someone walking past.

The piece was conceived, sourced and built within a single day. Rather than working towards a highly finished object, I wanted to explore what could happen when time, available materials and instinct shaped the design.

Design Brief

I set myself a simple but demanding challenge: find the materials, develop an idea and finish a welded sculpture inside 24 hours. The exercise was about rapid experimentation and ad hoc design rather than perfect planning or a refined, gallery-ready finish.

Working exclusively with metal that would otherwise have been discarded also meant that the forms of the available components had to guide what I could build.

Process

I began by searching through scrap metal and looking for pieces with character. A discarded pot suggested a head, and a pair of trailer springs suggested arms. Those discoveries provided the starting point for the seated, reading figure.

I worked without drawings. Instead, I held different pieces in position, stepped back to judge the proportions and tried alternatives as the form developed. Once a combination felt right, I welded it together and moved straight to the next decision. The sculpture emerged through a series of quick physical tests rather than a predetermined plan.

Outcome

I was surprised by how much I could make in a single day. The challenge showed me the value of material-led thinking, rapid prototyping and the willingness to commit to an idea before every detail has been resolved.

The trade-off was the finish: the welds, material choices and overall refinement are not at the level I would aim for in a more considered commission. More time would have improved those details, but the time limit was the point of the exercise. As an experiment in working quickly and responsively, it achieved exactly what I wanted to explore.
